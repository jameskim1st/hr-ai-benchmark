How BetterUp Built an AI-Only Coaching Product 95 Percent of Its Customers Love

Instead of speeding to get a new product to market, this coaching platform took its time to get its tech right.

Many tech companies have approached artificial intelligence with a “move fast and break things” mindset. But Alexi Robichaux, CEO and co-founder of enterprise coaching provider BetterUp, which was valued at $4.7 billion in 2021, says his team developed a winning AI product by doing the opposite.

Anyone can pick up their phone and ask ChatGPT, Gemini, or Claude for professional coaching and mental health advice. Robichaux knew that in order to be enterprise-grade, any AI-only coaching tool BetterUp created would need to “be better in terms of efficacy and outcomes” than popular chatbots. That’s why BetterUp didn’t release its AI coaching platform BetterUp Grow until this April—although the Austin-based company began developing the technology in 2021.

The first iteration of Grow was a voice-to-text product BetterUp internally called “Miranda,” according to Robichaux. Though the tool seemed “really cool” at the time, he says, it didn’t measure up to human coaches, so his team never launched Miranda.

Once GPT-4 came out in 2023, Robichaux recalls thinking the technology was finally sophisticated enough to allow BetterUp to create a standalone AI coaching offering. His team’s attention then turned to safety protocols and guardrails. Robichaux notes that his team spent most of their time on these aspects of the AI-only product because enterprises value knowing these tools are “trustworthy and safe” above all else.

The company announced Grow, after nearly two more years of development and testing, in January 2025, and then publicly launched it in April. Early testing seems promising; customers report 95 percent satisfaction rates and a 16 percent increase in confidence after using the tool, according to a company spokesperson.

Sales are growing too. Robichaux reports that BetterUp has seen “massive” member growth since debuting this product, as well as “a ton of interest” among existing members who use BetterUp’s other coaching tools. The company declined to share revenue metrics and profitability status.

“We care deeply about outcomes, we care deeply about doing this in safe and trusting ways,” Robichaux says. “And so we’ve spent a lot more time tuning and working on things internally before going to market.”
