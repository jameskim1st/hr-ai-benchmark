IBM lays off 8,000 to embrace AI — only to rehire just as many
May 23, 2025

IBM stunned the tech industry with a sweeping decision to lay off thousands of employees in favour of artificial intelligence (AI) automation. Yet, in an unexpected twist, the company’s headcount soon rebounded, offering a fresh perspective on how AI is reshaping the future of work.

In 2023, IBM made headlines by letting go of nearly 8,000 employees, primarily from its Human Resources division. The move was part of a strategic shift to automate routine administrative functions through an AI system known as AskHR. Designed to handle tasks such as holiday requests, payroll processing, and employee documentation, the AI platform was deployed to enhance efficiency and reduce operational costs.

The results were striking. AskHR automated around 94% of repetitive HR tasks, contributing to a reported $3.5 billion boost in productivity across more than 70 job roles. The initiative placed IBM alongside other major tech players such as Google and Spotify, which have similarly turned to automation to streamline support functions and optimise workflows.

Unexpected workforce growth following layoffs

Contrary to expectations, IBM’s overall workforce did not shrink. In fact, the company’s headcount grew after the initial redundancies. Chief Executive Officer Arvind Krishna explained the development in an interview with The Wall Street Journal, stating, “Our total employment has actually gone up, because what [AI] does is it gives you more investment to put into other areas.”

These areas include software engineering, marketing, and sales—roles that demand creativity, critical thinking, and direct human engagement.

Freed from routine administrative tasks, IBM reallocated resources to hire specialists in high-value disciplines. The company’s recruitment focus shifted towards technical, commercial, and client-facing functions—fields that remain less susceptible to automation. This approach reflects IBM’s broader strategy of integrating technological advancement with human expertise to drive innovation and business growth.

Redefining work in the age of AI

IBM’s experience is emblematic of a larger trend: AI is not merely eliminating jobs, but transforming them. As automation displaces routine roles, demand is rising for professionals who can design, implement, and manage AI-powered solutions.

Other companies have faced similar realities. Duolingo, for instance, experienced setbacks after heavily relying on chatbots, ultimately rehiring human staff when automation fell short. IBM’s case underlines the importance of upskilling and reskilling employees to navigate an evolving employment landscape.

AskHR, which managed over 11.5 million interactions in 2024, significantly improved internal service satisfaction. The platform’s net promoter score (NPS) climbed from -35 to +74 over several years—a testament to its growing acceptance. Still, approximately 6% of queries require human intervention, reinforcing the continued relevance of human judgement in complex or nuanced cases.

Striking a balance between innovation and human capital

IBM’s journey illustrates the delicate balance companies must maintain when adopting AI. While the technology promises significant productivity gains, it also necessitates a thoughtful approach to workforce transformation. As Krishna noted, automation has “allowed us to invest more in areas that need human creativity and interaction.”

Through the automation of routine functions, IBM has not only streamlined its HR processes but also reimagined how work is allocated across its global workforce of over 270,000 employees. The company’s evolving model offers valuable insights into how organisations can harness AI to both disrupt and create opportunities in the modern workplace. — IDR
